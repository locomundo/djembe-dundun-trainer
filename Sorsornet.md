# Sorsornet — transcription

Read off `notenblad-Sorsornet.pdf`. Audio in `audio/sorsornet-*.wav`. Legend as
in [Djole.md](Djole.md).

**Metre 12/8 — ternary.** Three subdivisions to the beat, counted **1 la li**.
Four beats to the bar = 12 slots.

**Cycle 2 bars = 24 slots.** The repeat sign splits the system in half and both
halves are written out, so the sheet shows the whole cycle.

The sheet marks **Einde** (Dutch, "end") on all seven parts at beat 4 of bar 2
— slot 21. There are no **Begin** marks.

---

## Signal

```
        1 la li   2 la li   3 la li   4 la li
 (T)    T  .  T   T  .  T   T  .  T   T  .  .
  ^galop
```

Both bars are identical. Each is led in by a **galop**: the grace note and the
stroke on the beat played as two very fast strokes, left then right. On the
sheet this is the little curl with a cue-sized notehead sitting exactly on
beat 1 — see the note on galops in [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md), because the cue
notehead is small enough to slip past the disc detector.

## Djembé 1 — two beats, repeated

```
 S  .  T   S  .  .
```

## Djembé 2 — two beats, repeated

```
 S  .  .   S  T  T
```

## Djembé 3 — two beats, repeated

```
 S  S  S   S  T  T
```

Every slot of beat 1 sounds. The densest djembe part on the sheet.

## Sangban — two beats, repeated

```
 drum   O  .  .   .  T  .
 bell   ✕  .  ✕   .  ✕  .
```

**The bell runs in twos against a beat of three.** It strikes every second
slot, so across two beats it falls on the beat, the **li**, then the **la** —
a three-against-two. It is the only bell on these sheets that does this, and
it is what makes Sorsornet's sangban worth singling out.

Both drum strokes sit on a bell stroke: open on beat 1, closed on the **la**
of beat 2.

## Kenkeni — one beat, repeated

```
 drum   .  .  T
 bell   ✕  .  ✕
```

One beat long. Bell on the beat, drum and bell together on the **li**.

## Doundounba — two bars

```
        1 la li   2 la li   3 la li   4 la li
 bar 1  T  T  T   .  .  .   .  .  T   T  T  .
 bell   ✕  ✕  ✕   .  ✕  .   ✕  .  ✕   ✕  ✕  .

 bar 2  T  T  T   .  .  .   .  .  .   .  .  .
 bell   ✕  ✕  ✕   ✕  .  ✕   ✕  .  ✕   ✕  .  ✕
```

A real phrase rather than a loop — the bell is **not** the same in both bars,
which is the only reason the cycle has to be 24 slots long. Nine drum strokes,
all of them doubled by the bell.

## Coincidence count

| part | drum | bell | drum on a bell stroke | bell only |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sangban | 8 | 12 | 8 (all) | 4 |
| kenkeni | 8 | 16 | 8 (all) | 8 |
| doundounba | 9 | 17 | 9 (all) | 8 |

No hand ever moves alone in any of the three parts. The difficulty is the
sangban's three-against-two bell, not the coordination.
