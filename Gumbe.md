# Gumbé — transcription

Read off `Gumbé.pdf`. Audio in `audio/gumbe-*.wav`. Legend as in [Djole.md](Djole.md).

**4/4 in sixteenths, two-bar cycle.** The only sheet here with **two sangban
parts**, and the only one whose call is not the shared Djolé call.

## Signal — all tones, no slaps

```
        1 e & a   2 e & a   3 e & a   4 e & a
  (T)   T . T T   . T . T   T . T .   T . . .
```

Three strokes, then two, then two, then one — thinning out across the bar.

## Djembé 1 — half bar

```
        1 e & a   2 e & a
        T T . S   T T S .
```

Two tones into a slap, twice — but the slap lands on the **a** the first time
and the **&** the second. That shift is the part.

## Djembé 2 — half bar

```
        1 e & a   2 e & a
        B . T T   . . S .
```

## Sangban 1 — half bar

```
  drum  O . . .   T . T .
  bell  ✕ . ✕ .   ✕ . ✕ .
```

## Sangban 2 — half bar

```
  drum  T T . .   O . . .
  bell  ✕ ✕ . ✕   ✕ . ✕ .
```

The two sangbans answer each other: **Sangban 1 has its open tone on beat 1,
Sangban 2 on beat 2**, and they run different bells.

## Kenkeni — one beat

```
  drum  T . . .
  bell  ✕ . ✕ .
```

## Doundounba — one bar

```
        1 e & a   2 e & a   3 e & a   4 e & a
  drum  O . . O   . . T .   T . T .   . . . .
  bell  ✕ . ✕ ✕   . ✕ ✕ .   ✕ . ✕ .   ✕ . ✕ .
```

Note the **bell changes halfway through the bar** — syncopated for two beats,
then straight eighths.

## Practising the dunduns

| | Bell | Drum | Bell-only |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sangban 1** | 16 | 12 | 4 |
| **Sangban 2** | 20 | 12 | 8 |
| **Kenkeni** | 16 | 8 | 8 |
| **Doundounba** | 20 | 10 | 10 |

Every drum stroke lands on a bell stroke in all four parts. Order:
**kenkeni → sangban 1 → sangban 2 → doundounba**, and leave the doundounba last
because of that mid-bar bell change.

With two sangbans running different bells, mute the one you are not learning —
otherwise you will drift into its bell.
