# Sunun — transcription

Read off `Sunun.pdf` (the file is titled *Mribayassa* internally). Audio in
`audio/sunun-*.wav`. Legend as in [Djole.md](Djole.md).

**4/4 in sixteenths, two-bar cycle.** Uses the shared Djolé call.

## Djembé 1 — one bar, **begins at bar 2 beat 4**

```
        1 e & a   2 e & a   3 e & a   4 e & a
        T . S S   . . S T   T . S S   B . S T
```

Nine strokes to the bar — the busiest djembe part across these sheets.

## Djembé 2 — half bar

```
        1 e & a   2 e & a
        S . . S   S . T T
```

**The same part as Fankani's Djembé 2** (and Toro's).

## Sangban — half bar, **begins at bar 2 beat 4**

```
  drum  T . . .   . . T .
  bell  ✕ . ✕ ✕   . ✕ ✕ .
```

## Kenkeni — one beat

```
  drum  T . . .
  bell  ✕ . ✕ .
```

## Doundounba — one bar, **begins at bar 2 beat 4**

```
        1 e & a   2 e & a   3 e & a   4 e & a
  drum  T . T T   . . O .   . . O .   O . . T
  bell  ✕ . ✕ ✕   . ✕ ✕ .   ✕ . ✕ .   ✕ . ✕ ✕
```

Seven strokes a bar mixing closed and **open** tones — and the bell doubles
every one of them.

## Practising the dunduns

| | Bell | Drum | Bell-only |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Doundounba** | 20 | 14 | **6** |
| **Kenkeni** | 16 | 8 | 8 |
| **Sangban** | 20 | 8 | 12 |

A useful reversal: **busier does not mean harder.** The doundounba has nearly
twice the strokes of the sangban and is the *easiest* of the three, because its
bell doubles the drum almost throughout. The sangban is the hardest — sparse,
off the beat, against a syncopated bell.

Order: **doundounba → kenkeni → sangban**.
